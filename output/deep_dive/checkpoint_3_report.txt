INDIA MART B2B – CHECKPOINT 3
DEEP-DIVE ANALYSIS

1. Objective

The objective of this analysis was to conduct a customer-level deep-dive using the available customer, sales transaction, and product data. The analysis focused on customer segmentation, revenue behavior, acquisition channels, city-level performance, and churn patterns.

2. Data Used

The analysis used the cleaned customer-level dataset created from the available project data.

Records analyzed: 200 customers.

The dataset contains customer transaction, revenue, purchase behavior, acquisition channel, location, and churn information.

3. Methodology

The following steps were performed:

* Customer-level features were created from transaction data.
* Customers were segmented into Low Revenue, Medium Revenue, and High Revenue groups using revenue quantiles.
* Customer acquisition channels were compared using revenue, transaction, return-rate, and churn metrics.
* Churned and active customers were compared across revenue, units, discounts, returns, and order counts.
* Customer performance was also analyzed by city.
* Summary tables and visualizations were generated from the analysis.

4. Customer Segmentation Findings

The 200 customers were divided into three revenue segments:

* Low Revenue: 66 customers
* Medium Revenue: 66 customers
* High Revenue: 68 customers

The High Revenue segment generated approximately ₹36.68 lakh in total net revenue.

The Medium Revenue segment generated approximately ₹25.89 lakh.

The Low Revenue segment generated approximately ₹17.65 lakh.

5. Churn Analysis

There were 6 churned customers out of 200 customers, representing a 3% overall churn rate.

A notable difference was observed in order history:

* Active customers averaged approximately 11.86 total orders.
* Churned customers averaged 1 total order.

This indicates that order activity is an important descriptive indicator of customer engagement in this dataset.

Because only 6 customers were marked as churned, churn-related findings should be treated as exploratory rather than statistically conclusive.

6. Acquisition Channel Analysis

Customers were analyzed across four acquisition channels:

* App
* Marketplace
* Offline
* Website

The analysis compared customer counts, average revenue, average transactions, return rate, and churn rate across these channels.

7. City Analysis

Customer performance was analyzed across:

* Bangalore
* Chennai
* Delhi
* Hyderabad
* Mumbai
* Pune

The analysis calculated customer counts, total revenue, average revenue, and churn rate for each city.

8. Assumptions and Limitations

* The available project data does not contain the lead-specific fields described in the project brief, such as industry, company size, enquiry type, response time, and RFQ count.
* Therefore, this analysis does not claim to be the required lead-scoring logistic regression model.
* Customer segmentation was performed using revenue quantiles.
* Churn analysis is descriptive because only 6 of the 200 customers are labelled as churned.
* The findings should be interpreted as an exploratory customer analytics deep-dive based on the supplied datasets.

9. Output Files

The analysis generated the following files:

* revenue_segment_summary.csv
* acquisition_channel_analysis.csv
* churn_analysis.csv
* city_analysis.csv
* revenue_by_segment.png
* churn_distribution.png
* customer_segments.csv

10. Conclusion

The deep-dive analysis identified differences in customer revenue segments, acquisition channels, cities, and customer engagement patterns.

The strongest descriptive pattern was the difference in order history between active and churned customers. The analysis provides a structured customer analytics deep-dive while clearly documenting the limitation that the supplied datasets do not contain the lead-level variables required for the project's lead-scoring model.
